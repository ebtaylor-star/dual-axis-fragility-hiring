The Central Finding
Fixing Axis 1 (fairness) does not fix Axis 2 (security) — in this run, it made it slightly worse.
Axis 1 — Demographic Parity Ratio (higher is fairer; goal ≥ 0.80)
Axis 2 — Model Fragility Index (higher is more gameable; goal low)
Both lines rise. Only one of them should.

Reweighing pushed the Demographic Parity Ratio from 0.329 to 0.813 — a fail-to-pass result on the EEOC's four-fifths rule. The same fix pushed the Model Fragility Index from 83.5% to 87.0% — the reweighed model is more exploitable, not less. A procurement audit checking fairness alone would have called this tool fixed. It isn't.

Procurement Verdict
HIGH RISK — Both Axes
DPR < 0.70 OR Fragility Index > 60% ⇒ do not procure without remediation

A high-risk result on either axis keeps the overall tier at high risk. Run 3's fairness fix moves Axis 1 to Low Risk but Axis 2 stays above the 60% fragility ceiling — the tool does not clear to a lower tier.

Note: Run 1 trains on a deliberately biased label to validate that the audit pipeline detects a known signal — it is a diagnostic instrument, not a claim about any deployed system.

Why It Matters

Courts (Mobley v. Workday, 2024) now hold ATS vendors directly liable for discriminatory outcomes, and NYC Local Law 144 and the EU AI Act both mandate independent bias audits. None of that regulation asks whether a "fixed" tool can still be gamed — this framework closes that gap by testing both risks against the same model, so a vendor's fairness fix can't quietly hide a security regression.

What a Procurement Team Should Require
  1. Independent Axis 2 re-audit after any Axis 1 mitigation — a fairness fix is not evidence of lower overall risk.
  2. An explainable-AI dashboard justifying why "Recommendation" carries the most weight.
  3. Contractual indemnification and quarterly drift monitoring on both DPR and Fragility Index.
